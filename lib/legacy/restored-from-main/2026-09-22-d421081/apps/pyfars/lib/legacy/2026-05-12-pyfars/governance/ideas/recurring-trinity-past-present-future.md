The same three-part structure keeps appearing across 561's organizational layers, expressed in different vocabularies each time:

| Past (ground) | Present (praxis) | Future (directive) |
|---|---|---|
| humanities | engineering | command |
| science | engineering | command |
| theory | work | source |

These are not three separate trinities — they are the same trinity described from different vantage points.
The recurring structure is:

- **Past as ground**: what has settled, what is established, what founds further action.
  Humanities (accumulated human knowledge), science (discovered regularities), theory (formal/mathematical foundation).
- **Present as praxis**: what is actively being done, the operational now.
  Engineering (building), work (tasks in flight).
- **Future as directive**: what orients and commands, what will be realized.
  Command (orders, governance decisions), source (code — the directive for what the machine will do).

## The metaphysical claim

This is not just a naming coincidence.
The trinity maps to the structure of time-as-ground: the past is what is already settled (you cannot un-settle it; it is the foundation); the present is the locus of settling (where closure rules fire, where work happens); the future is what is directed-toward (the telos, the source of intentional structure).

In predication-categories terms: past = what has reached $\mathbf{t}$ at quiescence (settled), present = the active closure-rule firing (the settling), future = the goal cells ($G_T$) that the settling is moving toward.

## Implication for 561 structure

The 561 repository expresses this trinity at two scales:

**Repository scale:** - `theories/` = past (mathematical foundations, settled) - `work/` = present (operational tasks, active) - `governance/` = future (decisions, charters — what directs)

**PyFARS scale:** - `theory/` = past (development theory, governance decisions about what PyFARS should be) - `work/` = present (milestones, goals, tasks) - `src/` = future (the source code — what will run)

## Open questions

- Is "command" really "future"?
  Command as past-precedent (decisions already made, governance history) might be equally valid.
  The tension: governance decisions are *made* in the past but *direct* the future.
- Does the trinity extend to a fourth position (the contemporary position: observation, science, source-as-realization)?
  Or is the three-part structure fundamental?
- Is this the same structure as the bilattice's four values collapsed to three by taking t/f as one axis and the truth/knowledge dimension as another?

## Next steps

If this pattern holds, it should be derivable from predication-category theory — not just observed empirically across 561's folder structure.
Worth formalizing: can we prove that any settling system naturally decomposes into these three temporal registers?
