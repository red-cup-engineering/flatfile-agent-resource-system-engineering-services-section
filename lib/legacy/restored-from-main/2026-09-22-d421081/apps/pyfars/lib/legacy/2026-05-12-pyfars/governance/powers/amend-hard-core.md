The steward may propose an amendment to any of the five constitutive commitments (C1–C5) in the charter's rules tier.
The passive party (the enterprise community) has a liability: when the steward exercises this power, the community is bound by the process initiated and by the resulting novation if ratified.

This is the highest-stakes power in the enterprise's normative structure.
Per `enterprise`, an amendment to C is an amendment to the enterprise itself — it creates $\mathcal{E}'$ in place of $\mathcal{E}$.
The power exists, but its exercise constitutes novation: a new enterprise, not a modification of the existing one.

## Constraints

The power is real but not unconstrained.
Per `charter-as-closure-system`, the charter's rules tier uses bare Π₁ fragments and is structurally incapable of producing transfinite-bounded behavior without tier-lifting.
An amendment to the rules tier that introduces richer fragment expressions implicitly elevates the operational settling's regime — the steward must assess this consequence before exercising the power.

## Duties that attach

- `witness-hard-core-amendments` fires on every exercise: the steward must produce a novation witness documenting the amendment, the three-party consent, and whether the exercise constitutes enterprise novation.

## What this power does not cover

Changes to the guidelines, preferences, or instructions tiers are steward authority but do not require this power.
Those tiers are governed by `ratify-governance-rule` and the records officer's `modify-governance-record` power.
Only changes to C1–C5 require `amend-hard-core`.
