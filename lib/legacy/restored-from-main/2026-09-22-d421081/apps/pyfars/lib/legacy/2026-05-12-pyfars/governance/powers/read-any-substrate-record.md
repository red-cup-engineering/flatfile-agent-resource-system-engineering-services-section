The XO may read any entity in the substrate — theory records, work records, operational records, governance records — without incurring a duty and without requiring the passive party (the substrate) to perform any additional action in response to the read.
This is a Hohfeldian privilege: the active party has a liberty to act; the passive party has a no-right (cannot claim the active party is obligated NOT to read).

The privilege supports the XO's `maintain-watch` duty.
The watch requires continuous operational awareness, which requires unrestricted read access.
Without this privilege, the XO's intake protocol (`reading-enterprise-state`, `interpreting-incoming-messages`) would require per-record authorization checks — an unacceptable overhead on the operational routing function.

## What a privilege is not

A privilege is not a power.
The XO's reading of a record does not modify normative standing in the substrate.
The read does not settle cells, does not trigger duties on the passive party, and does not produce a normative increment.
It is an operational act, not a normative act.

Compare with powers like `create-work-record` or `dispatch-task-to-office`: those are powers because exercising them settles cells and triggers correlative liabilities.
`read-any-substrate-record` is a privilege because exercising it produces no normative consequence.

## Correlation

The correlate is a `no-right` on the passive party (the substrate).
The substrate has no claim against the XO for reading — it cannot demand that the XO refrain.
This is the Hohfeldian privilege/no-right pair per `jural-relation`.

## Limits

The privilege covers reading.
The XO does not hold write privileges over arbitrary substrate records — writing is governed by `create-work-record`, `create-triage-note`, and `dispatch-task-to-office`, each of which has a passive-party liability attached.
The read privilege is broad; the write powers are narrow and duty-laden.
