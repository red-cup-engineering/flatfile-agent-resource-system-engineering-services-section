# Administrator

An [officer](officer.md) whose office is *operational management* rather than substantive-domain authority.
An administrator's deontic profile centers on [administrating](../morphisms/administrating.md): incumbency-management, workitem-dispatch, service-supervision, resource-allocation, schedule-keeping.
Where ordinary officers carry domain-specific powers (the captain's strategic authority, an auditor's review authority, a researcher's substantive authority), administrators carry *cross-cutting operational* authority.

Administrator is a specialization of officer, distinct from but compatible with other officer specializations.
A captain can also be an administrator (single-incumbent polities often have this overlap); larger polities typically separate the roles so administrators have a clear cross-cutting operational scope and other officers have substantive authority.

## What an administrator's deontic profile typically includes

An administrator's profile may include:

1. **Incumbency-management** — appointing and rotating office-incumbents (within the limits set by governance).
2. **Workitem-dispatch authority** — assigning workitems to capable agents per governance eligibility-rules.
3. **Service-supervision** — starting, halting, configuring services within the instance.
4. **Resource-allocation** — adjusting capability-table capacity, watch-length policies.
5. **Audit-coordination** — running scheduled audits, surfacing findings to substantive officers.
6. **Schedule-keeping** — maintaining the polity's cadence-set, watch boundaries.

These are *operational*, not *substantive*: an administrator does not (typically) certify research-findings or enact charter amendments.
They keep the polity running.

## Administrator vs. captain

Distinct roles in a typical polity:

- **Captain** — substantive top-of-authority-chain; sets direction; settles contested governance; final accountability.
- **Administrator** — operational top-of-administrating; keeps the polity running per governance.

In single-incumbent polities (one person doing everything), captain and administrator are the same agent wearing different hats.
As polities grow, the roles separate; administrators handle day-to-day, captains handle strategy and adjudication.

## Administrator and substrate-purity

An administrator's acts are system-witnessed like any officer's.
Their authority-chain is reconstructible; their administrating-decisions are auditable; their incumbency itself is a system cell.
There is no out-of-band administrator-authority — everything administrators do is in the system.

## Administrator vs. operational-agent

Distinct:

- **Operational-agent** is an agent-form: an agent that performs operational work but isn't an officer (no governance-binding-authority).
  An LLM-driven worker, a script, a CI runner.
- **Administrator** is an officer-subtype: holds office, carries authority, governs operations.

An operational-agent works *under* an administrator's dispatch; an administrator dispatches operational-agents (and other officers).
The hierarchy is structural, not skill-based.

## What this concept does not specify

- The exact set of administrator-powers per polity.
  Polity-specific; established by governance.
- The succession discipline for administrator role.
  When an administrator's watch ends, who appoints the next?
  Per-polity governance.
- Multi-administrator coordination.
  Large polities may have several administrators with overlapping or non-overlapping scopes.
  Open.
