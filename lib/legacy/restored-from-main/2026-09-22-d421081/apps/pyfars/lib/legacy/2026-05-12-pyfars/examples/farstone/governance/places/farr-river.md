The island's only meaningful watercourse.
Headwaters on Mt Farstone's southern flanks; descends through the upper forest, past the municipal well-field, through Farr Valley's farmland, and into Farston Harbor at the harbor's western edge.

The river's mid-course passes the **municipal well-field** — the recharge zone where the alluvial aquifer feeds the town's drinking-water wells.
Land use within the recharge zone is governed by the watershed-protection ordinance (logging restricted, septic standards heightened).

The riparian-buffer ordinance designates a 30-m vegetated buffer along both banks from the well-field down to tidewater; the buffer is observed by most landowners and ignored by two Farr Valley dairies (chronic compliance issue not currently in dispatch).

The river meets the sea at Farston Harbor; the freshwater plume on outgoing tides extends into the harbor and out past the breakwater.
The plume's extent is the boundary used for shellfish-closure decisions during heavy-rainfall events.
