The steward may dissolve an existing office from PyFARS's operative structure.
Dissolution removes the office's action-domain and deontic-profile from the active normative layer — incumbents lose the office's capability section, and the office's power/duty cells are revoked.

Per `delegation-as-authority-residuation`, revocation drives the office's jural-relation cells from $\mathbf{t}$ to $\top$ and then to $\mathbf{f}$ via the substrate's repair machinery.
Dissolution is the application of this revocation pattern to the entire office entity.

## Dissolution does not erase history

Per settling-monotonicity, the office's historical cells — the records of work done while the office was active, the incumbency records of past holders — are not retracted.
Dissolution asserts a $\mathbf{f}$-valued `active` cell on the office entity, which the Type N→O rules read as: this office no longer gates dispatch, no longer propagates capabilities on incumbency.
The past records remain.

## When dissolution is appropriate

- An office that has drifted into institutional corruption (doing external-goods work rather than advancing C) and cannot be recalibrated.
- An office whose function has been superseded by changes to O that make its scope redundant.
- Restructuring of O in response to a degeneration-mode 2 signal reported by the theory officer.

## Dissolution of functional vs. governing offices

Dissolving a governing office (steward, XO, theory-officer) is a constitutional act with enterprise-wide consequences:

- Dissolving the XO leaves all operational routing without a proper holder — an immediate operational risk.
- Dissolving the theory-officer means Γ-monitoring has no office; the steward must absorb that accountability directly.
- The steward cannot dissolve its own office without simultaneously constituting a successor steward (via novation), since dissolving the steward with no successor terminates the enterprise's normative structure.
