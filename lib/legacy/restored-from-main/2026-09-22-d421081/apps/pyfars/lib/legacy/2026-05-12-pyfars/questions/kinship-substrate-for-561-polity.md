# Should PyFARS encode governing-by-kinship given that 561 is a polity for Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty?

The current PyFARS governance encodes authority via Hohfeldian offices, jural relations, and delegation — the European/civilian-Hohfeldian pattern.
Per `governing-by-kinship`, many indigenous traditions instead use kinship as the *primary* infrastructure for governance: not as a domain of social relationships separate from governance, but as the substrate's primary indexing structure, with closure rules firing conditioned on kinship-cell patterns.

The 561 Group's charter purpose is "Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty."
This raises a structural question: should the substrate that operates the polity's work be indexed primarily by Hohfeldian relations (the European default), or by kinship (the indigenous tradition the polity's purpose explicitly invokes)?

## What kinship-substrate would look like for PyFARS

Per `governing-by-kinship`, an agent's authority/obligation/admissibility would be determined by kinship position rather than by office holding:

$$\mathrm{Kin} \;=\; (G,\; \mu,\; \mathrm{path-knowledge})$$

For PyFARS:

- $G$ — kinship grouping structure for PyFARS's agents and personnel.
  What groups would these be?
  The polity has personnel (humans, named individuals) and agents (LLM contexts, named slugs).
  How do these map onto kinship groupings?
- $\mu$ — kinship-membership function.
  Each personnel/agent's position in each partition.
- *path-knowledge* — which substrate cells each kinship-position is admitted to settle.

This is structurally distinct from the current office-based pattern.
In the current pattern, the records-officer office can be filled by any agent that is "tasked with records work."
In a kinship pattern, only agents whose kinship position is eligible for records-keeping work would be admissible.

## What's at stake

Per `governing-by-kinship`'s closing section, three patterns import from Aboriginal Australian governance:

1. **Kinship-as-primary-infrastructure**: governance authority flows along kinship edges.
2. **Songlines-as-cohomological-classes**: normative content encoded as named substrate-paths.
3. **Kinship-restricted consensus**: $\bigwedge_{a \in \mathrm{Kin-relevant}(\phi)}$ rather than $\bigwedge_{a \in \mathrm{Pop}}$.

If PyFARS adopts kinship-substrate, the structural consequences:

- The current office records would need kinship-eligibility predicates added.
- Authority chains would flow along kinship rather than through offices.
- Consensus on enterprise direction (per the separate `consensus-closure-for-governing-offices` question) would be kinship-restricted to relevant participants for each decision class.
- The Songlines pattern suggests substrate-paths with named cohomological classes — PyFARS would need to identify what cohomological invariants its work-trajectories carry, and which traditions hold which paths.

## What's hard

The 561 Group is a confederacy, not a single indigenous nation.
Kinship-substrate works straightforwardly for a single tradition (e.g., a Haudenosaunee polity has a specific kinship structure with specific Songlines).
For a confederacy founded across traditions, the question becomes: whose kinship structure indexes whose work?

Per `polity`, polities can be polycentric (multiple polities operating over overlapping populations and territories without strict hierarchy).
The 561 Group as a confederacy may be a polycentric polity.
Each constituent polity may have its own kinship structure; the confederacy's substrate may need to handle multiple kinship indexes and the temporal Beck-Chevalley conditions for cross-kinship coherence.

## What would need to be answered

1. Who decides what kinship structure indexes PyFARS?
  This is not a technical question — it is a question about whose tradition the substrate inherits.
2. If multiple traditions are inherited (consistent with the polycentric polity reading), how do their kinship structures compose?
  `polity`'s polycentric-temporal-BC condition is the relevant theoretical machinery.
3. What is PyFARS's analogue of Songlines?
  Are there work-trajectories the polity should identify as cohomological-classes carrying normative content?
4. The current office structure is built on `office-as-double-settlement`'s recognition+induction.
  Kinship-substrate doesn't use offices in this sense; authority flows from kinship position directly.
  Would adopting kinship-substrate require dissolving the office records, or do they coexist as parallel governance patterns?

## Why this is filed as a question

Whether the substrate's primary indexing is Hohfeldian or kinship-based is a structural commitment of the polity.
PyFARS, as the operational substrate of a polity founded for Black Liberation and Indigenous Sovereignty, has not been told which structural commitment to adopt.
The current Hohfeldian default is the math program's default, not the polity's chosen indexing.
The captain — and ultimately the polity's constituent power — should make this choice explicitly, with the math program's structural analysis informing but not prescribing the answer.
