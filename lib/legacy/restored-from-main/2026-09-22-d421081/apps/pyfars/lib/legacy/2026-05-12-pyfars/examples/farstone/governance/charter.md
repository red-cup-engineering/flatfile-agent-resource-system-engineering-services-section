Adopted by the citizens of Farston on April 12, 1923.
Last amended November 8, 2024.
This charter is the constitutive document of the city's governance.

## Article I — Hard-Core Commitments

These five commitments are constitutive of Farston as a municipality.
Amending any one of them requires a referendum of the citizens with two-thirds approval.

### C1 — Public Health and Safety

The city protects the health and safety of its residents.
Public water shall meet or exceed the technical standards declared in `specifications/water-quality-thresholds.md`.
Streets, bridges, and public buildings shall be maintained to the safety standards adopted by council ordinance.

### C2 — Fiscal Responsibility

The city operates within an annual budget approved by council.
No expenditure may be authorized that exceeds the available unencumbered balance of its line item without a council resolution amending the budget.
The finance director's monthly reconciliation reports are the substrate of record for all budget claims.

### C3 — Transparent Deliberation

All council deliberations and votes are public.
Meeting minutes are published within 5 business days of the meeting.
Citizens may petition the council under the procedure declared in Article III §2.

### C4 — Equal Treatment Under Local Law

Municipal services are delivered without preference based on residence within or outside named neighborhoods, except where the charter or a council ordinance establishes a service district.
The records clerk maintains the public registry of service districts.

### C5 — Jurisdictional Boundaries

Each municipal office acts within the jurisdiction defined by its charter section or council ordinance.
Acts outside an office's jurisdiction (ultra vires) are voidable by the council and produce an ultra-vires entry in the public record.

## Article II — Governance Structure

The city is governed by:

- **Mayor**: chief executive officer; serves a 4-year term; elected at-large.
- **City Council**: 5 members elected at-large to staggered 4-year terms; legislative authority.
- **Department Directors**: appointed by the mayor with council confirmation; report through the mayor; serve at-pleasure.
  - Public Works (water, sewage, roads)
  - Building & Safety (permits, inspections, code enforcement)
  - Finance (budget, treasury, reconciliation)
  - Records (clerk's office, public registries)
  - Fire (volunteer department, professional chief)
- **Citizens**: hold petition rights, vote in elections, may speak at council meetings.

## Article III — Procedural Rules

### §1 — Council quorum

A quorum is three members.
No motion passes without quorum.

### §2 — Citizen petition

A citizen may file a petition with the records clerk.
Petitions with 50+ signatures must be heard at the next regular council meeting; the council shall vote within two meetings of first hearing.

### §3 — Permit review window

Building permit applications shall receive a decision (approve, deny with reason, or request additional information) within 30 calendar days of complete-application receipt.
An application held past 30 days produces a charter-binding-violation entry in the public record.

### §4 — Quarterly water inspection

Public Works shall inspect every public hydrant and water-quality sampling point at least once per calendar quarter and file the results to the public registry.

### §5 — Budget cycle

The finance director presents a draft FY budget to the council by April 15 each year.
The council adopts a final budget by June 15.
Adopted budget is published immediately.

## Article IV — Amendment

This charter is amended by referendum.
The mayor or any council member may propose an amendment by motion adopted by majority of the council.
Adopted amendments are placed on the next regular election ballot.
A proposed amendment becomes effective if approved by two-thirds of voters.
