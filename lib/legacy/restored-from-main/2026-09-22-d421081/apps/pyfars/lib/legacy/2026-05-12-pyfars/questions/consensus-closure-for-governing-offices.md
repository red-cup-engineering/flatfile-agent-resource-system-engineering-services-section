# Should the parallel-governing-offices structure be modeled as consensus-closure rather than civilian-pluralism?

The decision `three-governing-offices-in-parallel` models PyFARS's three governing offices (steward, XO, theory-officer) using `military-command-chain-as-modal-composition`'s "civilian Hohfeldian pluralism" — three authorities operating in parallel under separate jurisdictions.
This is the Hohfeldian reading.

But the substrate situation may admit a `consensus-closure` reading instead: the three offices must align affirmatively for important enterprise actions to settle.
The steward proposes hard-core amendments only after the theory officer's degeneration signals, which depend on the XO's operational visibility.
Each office's effective authority depends on the others' affirmation of the prerequisites; none of them can act unilaterally on enterprise direction.

## What's at stake in the choice

Per `consensus-closure`, the universal-quantification antecedent ($\bigwedge$ across participants) is structurally cleaner than the civilian-pluralism reading's separate jurisdictions.
Civilian pluralism requires `jurisdiction-as-base-change` machinery and the temporal Beck-Chevalley condition for cross-jurisdiction coherence; consensus-closure requires only the bilattice's $\wedge_t$ operation across alignment cells.

If consensus-closure is the right model, the enterprise's important decisions settle by unanimous affirmation across the three governing offices, not by the captain's adjudication of parallel-jurisdictional outputs.
The captain's role becomes ratification of consensus, not coordination of separate authorities.

## What would the consensus-closure reading look like

For an enterprise direction $\phi$ (e.g., a hard-core amendment, a new office's installation, a milestone retargeting):

$$\langle \mathrm{steward}, \texttt{aligns-with}, \phi\rangle = \mathbf{t} \,\wedge\, \langle \mathrm{XO}, \texttt{aligns-with}, \phi\rangle = \mathbf{t} \,\wedge\, \langle \mathrm{theory-officer}, \texttt{aligns-with}, \phi\rangle = \mathbf{t} \;\Longrightarrow\; \langle \phi, \texttt{settled-by-governing-consensus}, \text{PyFARS}\rangle = \mathbf{t}$$

The captain's ratification then settles the consensus into binding policy.
Without consensus, the captain may still ratify by direct authority — but doing so produces a substrate-detectable cell pattern (consensus blocked, captain overrode), which differs structurally from consensus-ratified action.

## What would need to be answered

1. Which classes of enterprise actions are subject to the three-office consensus, and which remain unilateral powers (e.g., XO dispatch decisions, records officer record edits)?
2. How does the seventh-generation-projection extension (per `consensus-closure`) interact with PyFARS's Γ regime classification?
  Should some enterprise decisions require seven-generation projection?
3. When consensus blocks indefinitely, what is the substrate-algebraic move?
  `consensus-closure` names three options (continued deliberation, clan-mother removal, issue setting-aside); which of these has analogues in PyFARS?
4. Is the current `three-governing-offices-in-parallel` decision wrong, or is it correct *for the operational layer* while consensus-closure is correct *for the enterprise-direction layer*?
  The two are not necessarily exclusive.

## Why this is filed as a question, not a decision

Choosing between civilian-pluralism and consensus-closure is a structural commitment with cohomological consequences (per `consensus-closure`'s closing section: "consensus and majority governance have cohomologically distinguishable substrates").
The choice should be made by the captain after considering both readings, not unilaterally by the XO writing governance records.
The current `three-governing-offices-in-parallel` decision should be re-examined in light of this question.
